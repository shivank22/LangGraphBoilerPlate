## Research question
Research Vastu Shastra: what it is, its historical roots/texts, key concepts (elements, directions, mandala), common applications, and what credible sources say about evidence/criticism.

## Findings by sub-topic

### 1) Core idea & purpose
Vastu Shastra is commonly described as a traditional Indian (Hindu) system of architecture/planning concerned with how buildings and spaces are laid out and oriented. Wikipedia summarizes it as a design approach that aims to align spaces with natural forces (e.g., sunlight and wind) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vastu_shastra).

### 2) Key concepts: five elements (Pancha Bhootas)
Many modern explanations present Vastu as balancing five elements—earth, water, fire, air, and space. Morphogenesis explicitly lists these “Pancha Bhootas” as the five elements used in Vastu discussions (https://www.morphogenesis.org/media/vastu-pancha-bhootas/).

### 3) Vastu Purusha Mandala (the grid/diagram)
A central planning concept is the Vastu Purusha Mandala: a square grid/mandala used to map a site/building and relate zones to directions and symbolic/cosmic principles. Morphogenesis describes the Vastu Purusha Mandala as a geometric grid used in Vastu Shastra to plan/design buildings (https://www.morphogenesis.org/media/vastu-pancha-bhootas/). Kaarwan likewise describes it as a symbolic square grid representing the layout of a structure (https://www.kaarwan.com/blog/architecture/concept-of-vastu-purush-origins-significance-in-architecture?id=858).

### 4) Historical texts & tradition
Vastu is linked to a broader set of classical architectural treatises. WisdomLib’s overview of “South Indian Vastu Texts” notes architectural texts like Mayamata and Manasara (https://www.wisdomlib.org/concept/south-indian-vastu-texts). Google Books describes Samarangana Sutradhara as an encyclopedic work on classical Indian architecture (Vastu Shastra) written by King Bhoja (1000–1055 AD) (https://books.google.com/books/about/Samarangana_Sutradhara.html?id=n5szLgEACAAJ).

### 5) Evidence, criticism, and modern claims
A common criticism is that many Vastu claims are not supported by scientific evidence and can function as superstition/pseudoscience in practice. The Maharashtra Andhashraddha Nirmoolan Samiti presents a skeptical critique of Vastu as “pseudo-science” (https://antisuperstition.org/false-notion-of-vastushastra/). (Note: this is an advocacy/rationalist source, not an academic peer-reviewed evaluation; it’s useful as an example of prominent criticism.)
